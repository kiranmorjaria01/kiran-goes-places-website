The W Trek in Patagonia, Without a Single Word

The short version

  • Told entirely through trail sound and scenery, no narration, across the granite ridge, the towers at golden hour, and Lago Grey.
  • The trail alternates between open granite ridges and dense lenga forest, each with a completely different kind of quiet.
  • The trek ends on the Grey Glacier itself with crampons and a guide, then a boat back through icebergs that have calved off the glacier.

I've made a lot of videos where I talk over every single shot. This one didn't feel like it needed that. Some scenery just does the talking for you, so this is the W Trek with no narration, just trail sound, wind, and whatever was actually in front of the camera.

Kiran Morjaria

13 February 2026

Updated 18 August 2026

Torres del Paine has a way of making conversation feel unnecessary. I still narrate most of my videos, but this trail earned a quieter edit.

The ridge line out of camp

Early on, the trail climbs a bare ridge with the valley dropping away on one side, no forest cover yet, just gravel underfoot and whatever weather is rolling in. It's the kind of stretch where you stop noticing your own footsteps.

No forest cover yet, just the ridge and whatever weather is coming in.

The towers at golden hour

This is the shot that's on every postcard of Chilean Patagonia, and it still isn't what you expect in person. The light catches the granite spires and turns them almost orange while the lake below goes still enough to double the whole scene. No amount of narration adds anything to this one.

A glacial lake, up close

Further along the trail, the water shifts to this turquoise you only really get from glacial meltwater, fed straight off the ice above. Standing at the edge of it, the scale of the cliffs around you only really registers once you've got a person in the frame for reference.

Glacial meltwater, and a lot more scale than the photos suggest.

Into the lenga forest

The trail dips constantly between open granite and dense lenga forest, bare white trunks packed tightly enough that the light barely gets through. It's a completely different kind of quiet to the ridge lines, closer in, muffled, almost indoors.

Dense lenga forest, a different kind of quiet to the open ridges.

Somewhere along Lago Grey

Days in, following the lake out towards Grey Glacier, the weather turns properly overcast and the whole valley goes a flat, moody blue. It's one of those moments the camera almost undersells, arms out, glacier visible in the distance, nothing left to say about it.

Lago Grey, glacier visible in the distance, nothing left to say.

Onto the ice itself

The trek ends with crampons and a harness, out onto the Grey Glacier itself with a guide leading the way between crevasses. The blue gets deeper the further you go in, and there's a constant sound of running meltwater underfoot even when you can't see it.

Crampons on, rope out, deeper into the blue.

Icebergs from the boat back

The way out is by boat, weaving between chunks of ice that have calved off the glacier and drifted down the lake. Some are small, some are the size of a house, and all of them are a blue that doesn't quite photograph the way it looks in person.

Calved off the glacier, drifting down the lake.
