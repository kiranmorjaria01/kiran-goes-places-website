Destination Guide

Colombia Travel Guide

Two to four weeks covers Colombia comfortably: the Caribbean coast around Cartagena and Tayrona, the coffee region around Salento, Medellín and its surrounding hills, and the birdwatching town of Minca, with Bogotá usually last as a cooler, higher altitude finish before flying home. It rewards slowing down between stops rather than rushing between them, three or four days per base feels about right, and the extra time consistently goes on coffee, street art, salsa lessons and the odd unplanned detour with someone you met an hour earlier.

Common Questions

How long do I need in Colombia?

Two to four weeks covers the main route comfortably: the Caribbean coast around Cartagena and Tayrona, the coffee region around Salento, Medellín, and Bogotá as a final stop. Three or four days per base is enough to see the highlights without rushing between them.

What's Comuna 13 in Medellín actually like?

Once one of the most dangerous neighbourhoods in the world during the cartel era, Comuna 13 is now known for its murals, open air escalators and street art, alongside guided tours that are honest about the darker history rather than glossing over it.

When does Tayrona National Park close?

Tayrona closes completely for about a month every year, usually in February, to let the park rest. Outside that window it's open as normal, with hammocks over the sea among its main draws.

What do I need to know before visiting Tayrona?

There are two entrances that matter, you'll need to carry your passport to get in, hammocks creak all night if that's how you choose to sleep, and there's a currents warning sign at the beach that's worth taking seriously rather than ignoring.

What's Salento known for?

Salento is Colombia's coffee country: horseback rides through the Cocora Valley, coffee farms where you pick the cherries by hand, and tejo, a national sport played with gunpowder and beer. It's one of the most relaxed stops on a Colombia trip.

Is Bogotá worth visiting if I'm short on time?

Even one day is worth it. Most travellers skip Bogotá or just pass through, but La Candelaria, the Botero Museum and the Gold Museum, home to over 50,000 pre-Hispanic gold pieces, make a strong case for stopping rather than flying straight past it.

What's Minca, and is it worth the detour?

Minca is a mountain town tucked into Colombia's Sierra Nevada, known for birdwatching, waterfalls hidden in the forest and a sunset viewpoint with a live band. It's a quieter, cooler contrast to the Caribbean coast just down the hill.
