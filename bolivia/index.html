Destination Guide

Bolivia Travel Guide

Bolivia's biggest draw is the Uyuni Salt Flats, the largest in the world and, for a few months each year, turned into a perfect mirror by a thin layer of rainwater. La Paz, the world's highest de facto capital, makes a good stop either side of it: cable cars instead of buses, and a Witches' Market thrown in.

Quick Facts

Main regions covered

La Paz, the Uyuni Salt Flats, the Atacama-to-Uyuni road trip

Best season

May to October for full dry-season access and clear skies, or January to March if the mirror effect is the priority, since it's the thin layer of rainwater that creates it.

Language

Spanish

Currency

Bolivian boliviano (BOB)

Daily budget

$30-50 (independent/backpacker travel)

Recommended trip time

5-7 days: a couple of days in La Paz to acclimatise, then a 3-day tour to and around the Uyuni Salt Flats.

Common Questions

When's the best time to see the Uyuni Salt Flats mirror effect?

January to March is the rainy season, when a thin layer of water spreads across the salt crust and creates the mirror effect, sky reflected perfectly across the plain. May to October is the dry season instead, flat and mirrorless but more settled and predictable.

How do you get from Chile to the Uyuni Salt Flats?

One option is a three-day 4x4 road trip from Atacama, Chile, crossing the border early morning and taking in pink flamingo lagoons, geysers at 5,000m and an actual train cemetery along the way, typically running $150 to $210 covering transport, guide and meals.

What should I wear on the Uyuni Salt Flats?

Waterproof footwear is non negotiable in the wet season, since water spreads across huge areas and even a short walk from the jeep will soak and salt-crust your shoes. Temperatures also swing hard, so pack for both extremes.

Is it worth staying in a salt hotel?

Yes, if you can. Walls, floors, tables and beds carved entirely from blocks of salt on the edge of the flats make for one of the more memorable nights on a Bolivia trip, even if extras like an en suite bathroom can cost separately.

What's La Paz like?

South America's most underrated city, according to the trip covered here: cable cars instead of buses, dried llama fetuses at the Witches' Market, and $10 fine dining tasting menus using ingredients like quinoa, pumpkin, plantain and giant corn.

Do I need to acclimatise before visiting Bolivia's high altitude areas?

Yes. Spending a few nights acclimatising somewhere high beforehand, Uyuni town or Atacama both work, staying hydrated and taking it slow while exploring is the advice that comes up repeatedly at this altitude.
