Peru Travel Guide

A Peru trip built around Cusco, the Salkantay Trek and Machu Picchu needs at least a week once travel days are counted. Cusco alone deserves two or three days to acclimatise and explore the Sacred Valley, the Salkantay Trek itself runs four days across glacial passes and cloud forest, and Machu Picchu is worth a full day on top of that, longer if Rainbow Mountain's colours and crowds are also on the list.

Quick Facts

Suggested trip length

2-3 days in Cusco, 4 days on the Salkantay Trek, at least a day at Machu Picchu

Main regions covered

Cusco, the Sacred Valley, the Salkantay Trek, Machu Picchu, Rainbow Mountain

Currency

Peruvian sol

Rough cost

From around $250pp for the Salkantay Trek and Machu Picchu leg, travelling independently

Common Questions

How long do I need for Cusco, the Salkantay Trek and Machu Picchu?

Budget at least a week all in: two or three days in Cusco to acclimatise and see the Sacred Valley, four days on the Salkantay Trek itself, and at least a full day at Machu Picchu. Add more if Rainbow Mountain is also on the list.

Is the Salkantay Trek hard?

Altitude is the biggest challenge, climbing to 4,630m before descending towards Machu Picchu, and it's generally considered harder than the classic Inca Trail. The weather is properly unpredictable too, blue skies one stretch, closing in the next, especially in the mornings.

Do I need a guide for the Salkantay Trek?

No, it can be done self-guided, though a shared guide runs about $25 a person and a private guide $80 plus. Comfort varies massively by tour company if you do book one, and horses can carry your gear if you go guided.

How much does the Salkantay Trek and Machu Picchu actually cost?

Most independent travellers end up spending at least $250 a person once entrance tickets, the mountain hike ticket, buses, the train and accommodation in Aguas Calientes are all added up. Hotels there range from around $40 to $200 a night.

Is Rainbow Mountain worth it if it's cloudy?

It's a genuine risk. Rainbow Mountain sits at 4,900m and its colours, caused by mineral layers of iron, copper and sulphur, can be completely swallowed by cloud on the day. It's still worth the early 3am bus from Cusco, but there are no guarantees.

Is Cusco worth more than a stopover before Machu Picchu?

Yes. Cusco is the gateway to Machu Picchu, but the city and the surrounding Sacred Valley deserve two or three days of their own rather than being treated as a one-night stopover on the way to the trek.
