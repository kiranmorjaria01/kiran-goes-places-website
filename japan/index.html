Japan Travel Guide

Five days is enough for a solid first look at Tokyo, hidden bars in Shinjuku, sumo in Ryogoku, cherry blossoms in Asakusa and enough neon to last a lifetime, though it barely scratches the surface of a city that's really 23 different ones stitched together. Add a day trip out to Lake Kawaguchiko for proper Mount Fuji views, and build in flexibility, cherry blossom season and events like the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka are worth timing a trip around if either is a priority.

Common Questions

How many days do I need in Tokyo?

Five days gives a genuinely solid first look, though Tokyo is really 23 different districts stitched together, so it can easily absorb more time. Add a day trip to Lake Kawaguchiko if Mount Fuji views are a priority.

Which viewpoint is best for seeing Mount Fuji from Lake Kawaguchiko?

Not every viewpoint around the lake is worth the walk. Chureito Pagoda is the standout, a five hundred year old pagoda with Fuji behind it, alongside spots like Oishi Park, the Kachi Kachi ropeway and even a Lawson convenience store car park with a surprisingly good angle.

Is Tokyo hard for vegetarians?

It has a reputation as a hard city for vegetarians, but that isn't true anymore. There's genuinely good vegetarian food across the city, from honey mustard gyoza to ramen, across a five day trip's worth of eating.

What's worth doing in Tokyo beyond the obvious sights?

Among 20 highlights: fighting a sumo wrestler, go-karting through the streets, Shibuya Sky's rooftop views, a whiskey bar the size of a living room, and a day trip out to Lake Kawaguchiko, alongside the more expected Shibuya Crossing and Senso-ji Temple stops.

Is it worth going to Japan for the Suzuka Grand Prix?

If motorsport is a genuine interest, yes. One trip here flew in a week early with no ticket and no plan, then drove a tiny kei car across the country to reach the race, during cherry blossom season.

What currency does Japan use?

The Japanese yen. Costs vary hugely by what you're after, retro game shops and arcades are affordable, while areas like Ginza carry some of the most expensive real estate on the planet, so budgets stretch a long way if you avoid those pockets.
